URA Stage Name Rules and Conventions

Giving a Stage Name to an umamusume is governed by a strict blend of regulatory rules enforced by the Umamusume Racing Association (URA).

Definition and Purpose

A Stage Name is a specialized pseudonym assigned to an Umamusume by the URA. Its main objective is to protect the Umamusume’s personal safety and privacy by keeping her true identity hidden from the public eye.

Assignment
The stage name is assigned during administrative procedures when an Umamusume decides to pursue a racing career.
Identity protection
This strict identity-protection framework includes withholding the surname of certain trainers.
Business Name
When marrying an Umamusume, the husband must establish a “Business Name” for himself, to be used in any future official proceedings involving a potential Umamusume child.
The Rare Rule
(established in 1988)
In the rare event that a human mother gives birth to an Umamusume, she is assigned a “Stage Name” following the same administrative protocol as a father’s business name assignment.
Ex: Rare Chance

The Strict Regulatory Rules

Before an umamusume can race, her stage name must be submitted by the parents and approved by URA. The rules fall into two main categories: character limits and content restrictions.

Character and Language Restrictions

Katakana character limit
A stage name must be between 2 and 9 katakana characters long. Single-character names, or names exceeding the character limit, are strictly prohibited.
English registration limit
For international registrations and foreign broadcasts, the katakana name is transcribed into English (Roman alphabet). The English version cannot exceed 18 characters, including spaces.

Content and Historical Restrictions

No famous umamusume names
You cannot reuse the stage names of famous umamusumes or internationally protected stage names.
Name reuse cooldown
If an umamusume was not famous, her stage name cannot be reused until at least 4 full calendar years have passed following the year the original umamusume was deregistered.
No direct parent duplication
An Umamusume cannot share the exact stage name of her mother, nor the exact business name of her father.
No commercial branding
Stage names cannot include trademarked company names, commercial brands, or explicit promotional material.
No obscenity or offense
Stage names that are vulgar, offensive, or politically or religiously controversial are rejected.
Name change limits
An umamusume’s stage name can only be changed once in her lifetime, and only if she has never run in an official race. Once an umamusume runs her first race, the name is locked forever.

Naming Patterns & Traditions

Crown Names

Many prominent individual families use a fixed prefix or suffix (a “crown name”) across all their umamusumes for affiliation, combining it with a second word for individual identity, such as the Mejiro family and the Symboli family.

Pedigree and Heritage Blends

Blending parts of the Mother’s Stage Name and Father’s Business Name together.

[Mother’s first “Stage Name”] + [Father’s second “business name”]
